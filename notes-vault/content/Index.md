# Cybersecurity Knowledge Base

This website is a collection of my personal notes, research, and practical labs in cybersecurity. It documents the concepts, techniques, and tools I study while developing skills in both offensive and defensive security. Many entries originate from hands-on labs, CTF challenges, and independent research.

The goal of this knowledge base is to serve as a structured reference for learning, experimentation, and continuous improvement in cybersecurity.

> [!note] Quick Word
> Every open port is a question. Every suspicious log entry is a possible lead. Every service running on an unexpected port is an invitation to investigate — and occasionally, an invitation to spend the next four hours wondering why nothing works.
>
> The documentation is just as unpredictable. Some pages are polished walkthroughs with clear explanations and working commands. Others are rough field notes, incomplete investigations,  abandoned ideas, and fragments of reasoning written at hours when sleep was clearly the better option. A few entries may look like they were written by someone who understood the problem perfectly at the time and then immediately forgot how language works.
>
> Not everything here is finished. Some machines have been fully compromised, documented, and retired with dignity. Others are still sitting somewhere between initial enumeration and privilege escalation, waiting for me to return and finish what I started. A few are probably laughing at me.
>
> You'll find commands that worked on the first attempt, commands that refused to work for reasons I still don't understand, and troubleshooting sessions that somehow became longer than the actual lab. Occasionally, a command will be documented without enough context. Occasionally, there will be a typo. There are rabbit holes that led to genuine discoveries and others that led absolutely nowhere — except, perhaps, to a better understanding of what not to do.
>
> Professional documentation? Sometimes.
>
> If a note looks incomplete, it probably is. If you find a typo, consider it an undocumented feature.
>
